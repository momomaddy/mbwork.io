01 · Frontline Instincts

The Arcade In The Valley

Before I ever wrote a line of corporate positioning, drafted speeches for founders, or architected multi-billion dollar crisis responses, I learned commerce where it hurts: with real people in real rooms.

At sixteen, my friends and I pooled our travel money, borrowed from my father, and opened a video games parlour in Kashmir. It failed financially, yet it sparked a gaming and hangout ritual that continues in our 100,000-person town to this day. The machinery didn't make it work—the gathering ritual did. It proved that people don't buy products; they buy permission to participate.

Early archives & notebook drafts, 2013–2016.
Field observations & manuscript margins.
02 · The Scottish Discipline

Say Less, Mean More

Studying economics in Edinburgh and working on the frontlines taught me the British economy of language. Waiting tables during Six Nations rugby clashes and conducting street-level focus groups for Police Scotland's public awareness campaigns taught me that audiences tell you what they care about with everything except their words.

If corporate communications sounds like an institutional boardroom memo, it dies on arrival. True strategic communication requires radical empathy: peeling away corporate jargon until only undeniable truth remains.

Edinburgh & the Scottish restraint in narrative.
In the field: brand activations and direction.
03 · The Enterprise Crucible

Narrative Under Pressure

In hypergrowth at OYO, I led communications during international expansions where narrative drift had to be stopped before it started. At hear.com across APAC, we turned medical prosthetics into lifestyle wellness gear, driving high-ASP category adoption ahead of a $2B Nasdaq filing.

And at Adani Group—navigating 20+ businesses across airports, utility, green energy, and high-visibility corporate scrutiny—I directed communications that reached over 400M+ views, anchoring multi-billion dollar industrial infrastructure into human, community-level progress.
