Early Practice

Before I wrote my first line of copy or designed a corporate communications architecture, I sold directly to people on the front lines.

At sixteen, my friends and I pooled our trip money, and I borrowed from my father to open a video games parlour. It failed, yet it started a gaming and hangout culture that thrives to this day in our small town of 100K people. I wanted my hands on every medium to understand what brought people together.

That Curiosity Is Still How I Work

I grew up in Kashmir—a place where beauty and contradiction rarely arrive separately. Edinburgh taught me the complementary discipline: say less, mean more.

Somewhere in the intersection of culture, human observation, and digital craft is where all of this meets. Writing fiction and essays helps me capture people as they are; strategy helps institutions articulate what they mean.

things i've learned came from the rooms i've worked in:

  • a marketing agency & PR boutique: everyone should work in one at least once—to learn whether it is meant to be or not, and how quickly messages travel.
  • waitstaff & frontline retail: waiting tables at Six Nations rugby games and handling grocery rush hours taught me that people are always telling you what matters—rarely with words, usually without them.
  • public institutions & youth clubs: running focus groups across Edinburgh with Police Scotland proved that real listening happens when you drop the jargon entirely.
  • a hypergrowth startup (OYO): to build clarity in the middle of chaos, make decisions quickly, and fix narrative drift before it spreads.
  • complex utility & green infrastructure (Adani): an unfamiliar, technical domain is daunting until you step into it—and then it teaches you what conventional brand marketing never could.

observing, taking notes, and finding clarity across different rooms.
