Biography

The work behind the work.

Before I was a marketer, or wrote my first copy, I sold to people. Before I built campaigns, I answered customer questions directly on the front lines.

At sixteen, my friends and I pooled our trip money, and I borrowed from my father to open a video games parlour. It failed, yet it started a gaming and hangout culture that thrives to this day in our small town of 100K people.

Later, I sold educational books, waited tables at Six Nations Rugby games, and answered customer queries at McDonald's and Waitrose—followed by Police Scotland, running focus groups in schools and youth clubs across Edinburgh. Looking back, none of those jobs were really different. People were always telling me what mattered—sometimes with words, usually without them.

"Figure out what an audience believes before a campaign starts, and what they should believe when it ends. Everything else is execution."

I grew up in Kashmir—a place where beauty and contradiction rarely arrive separately. Edinburgh taught me the opposite instinct: say less, mean more. Somewhere between those places I developed the only skill that's consistently mattered in this work: paying attention. Campaigns begin in dashboards; brands begin in observation.

Over the last decade, I've worked across infrastructure, hospitality, AI, healthcare, fintech, and consumer technology, translating complex businesses into stories people understand and remember. Outside work, I'm building EH5 Stories and writing my first novel.
