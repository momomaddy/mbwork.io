01 · Early Practice

Learning what moves people

Before I wrote my first line of corporate narrative or architected multi-market campaigns, I was trying to understand why people gather in rooms.

At sixteen, my friends and I pooled our trip money, and I borrowed from my father to open a video games parlour. It failed commercially, yet it catalyzed a gaming and hangout culture that endures to this day in our small town of 100,000 people. I wanted my hands on every medium to explore what brings communities together.

02 · Creative Tension

That curiosity is still how I work

I grew up in Kashmir—a place where extraordinary beauty and historical contradiction rarely arrive separately. Living and studying economics in Edinburgh taught me the complementary discipline: say less, mean more.

Somewhere at the intersection of culture, human observation, and digital craft is where all of this meets. Writing fiction and essays helps me capture people as they are; strategy helps institutions articulate what they mean.

Things I've learned came from the rooms I've worked in:

  • A Marketing Agency & PR Boutique: Everyone should work in one at least once—to learn whether it suits them, and how quickly messages travel under pressure.
  • Waitstaff & Frontline Retail: Waiting tables during Six Nations rugby games and handling grocery rush hours taught me that people are always signaling what matters—rarely with direct words, usually without them.
  • Public Institutions & Youth Clubs: Running focus groups across Edinburgh with Police Scotland proved that real listening begins only when you drop corporate jargon entirely.
  • A Hypergrowth Startup (OYO): Building clarity in the middle of international scaling, making decisions quickly, and fixing narrative drift before it spreads.
  • Utility & Energy Infrastructure (Adani): Technical domains are daunting until you step inside—and then they teach you what conventional consumer marketing never could.

Observing, taking notes, and finding clarity across different rooms.
