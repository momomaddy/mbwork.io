The Challenge

Overcoming scattered updates.

As OYO scaled across geographies and functions, internal communication became inconsistent. The fix was not frequency. It was segmentation and purpose.

The system needed to separate high-speed news updates from deep contextual explainers and executive media intelligence—ensuring every tier of the organization received the right information at the right cadence.

Architecture

Three segmented internal channels.

OYO Shorts

Fast, high-frequency awareness updates on launches, news, and active movement across markets.

OYO Tinyletter

Readable explainers translating business activity into organizational meaning and strategic context.

The OYO Press

Leadership media-intelligence layer tracking press coverage, public perception, and narrative direction.

This structure separated awareness, understanding, and executive visibility—improving message clarity across employee, manager, and stakeholder audiences.

Role Summary

What I led across OYO initiatives.

Concept & Copy: Concept ideation and copywriting across digital, social, OOH, and in-store formats.
Messaging Architecture: Communication strategy and messaging architecture across launches and cross-functional programs.
Program Leadership: Program leadership across stakeholder management, media coordination, operations, and narrative consistency.
Data Integration: Integration of data, content, and execution to make communication measurable and culturally resonant.

System Takeaway

Internal comms in hypergrowth isn't about sending more emails—it's about building structured channels for awareness, context, and executive perception.
